# UV-Control v0.4: design notes

**Status (2026-11-08): first-draft schematic. Not yet prototyped, laid out or
fabricated.** The PCB file still holds the v0.1_A+ layout.

## What changes from v0.1

| | v0.1 | v0.4 |
|---|---|---|
| Emitter and detector | plug into sockets on the board (cuvette holder) | **JST PH 6 cables**, so the holder can be tightened |
| UV LED supply | 555-based boost circuit on the board | **8 V LMR62014 boost** on the board (U1) |
| Microcontroller | Adafruit ItsyBitsy nRF52840 | **Pro Micro nRF52840** (SuperMini / nice!nano clone) |
| Display | SSD1306 OLED | **2.0 in ST7789 240×320 IPS TFT** on an 8-wire cable |
| Analog inputs | ItsyBitsy ADC | **ADS1115** (16-bit, 4 channels), on the board |

The ADS1115 is needed because the Pro Micro has only three analog-capable
pins (P0.02, P0.29, P0.31), and the board reads four signals.

## Pin map

The Pro Micro is numbered like a DIP: pins 1–12 down the left side (USB at the
top), 13–24 up the right side, plus B+ (25) and B− (26) on either side of the
USB-C connector. The numbering and footprint follow Rook v0.4, which uses these
boards. CircuitPython names come from its `SuperMini NRF52840` build.

| Pin | nRF52840 | CircuitPython | Net | Goes to |

U3 is the bare ADS1115IDGS (VSSOP-10, 0.5 mm pitch; DigiKey ADS1115IDGST).
Its support parts: C11 100 nF at VDD, R9/R10 4.7 kΩ I2C pull-ups to 3.3 V
(the Pro Micro has none), and R11 10 kΩ on ALERT/RDY, which is open-drain.
ADDR goes to GND, giving address 0x48. The 12-bit ADS1015IDGS fits the same
footprint; the firmware would then use `ADS1015` instead of `ADS1115`.

ADS1115 channels, each through a 4.7 kΩ + 100 nF filter (R5–R8, C7–C10):

| Channel | Signal | From |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | detect | detector, J1-5 |
| A1 | rect | detector, J1-4 |
| A2 | light-tight | detector, J1-3 |
| A3 | LED current sense (V ÷ 24.9 Ω = LED current) | emitter, J2-1 |

## Power

5 V comes in on J5. The Pro Micro runs from its own USB-C. (JP1 could link RAW
to 5 V, but RAW is most likely the battery pin; see below.) It feeds the
detector, the emitter's op-amp, U1 (the 8 V boost for the UV LED) and U2, an
AP2112K-3.3 regulator for the TFT and the ADS1115.

## 8 V boost for the UV LED

U1 is a TI LMR62014 (SOT-23-5, 1.6 MHz), with values from its datasheet
(SNVS735B). The UV LED drops about 6 V and the emitter's current sense about
0.65 V, so 8 V leaves the emitter's current regulator about 1.3 V to work with.
The emitter regulates the LED current itself, so ripple on this rail matters
little; R15 and C5 filter it anyway.

| Ref | Value | Part / notes |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | LMR62014XMF | SOT-23-5: 1 SW, 2 GND, 3 FB, 4 EN (SHDN, high = on), 5 VIN |
| L1 | 10 µH | shielded, 4 × 4 mm (Bourns SRN4018 footprint), saturation current ≥ 1 A |
| D1 | MBR0520 | 20 V 0.5 A Schottky, SOD-123 (the datasheet's choice) |
| C12 | 10 µF | input, X5R/X7R, ≥ 10 V |
| C13 | 10 µF 25 V | output, X5R/X7R, 1206 (ceramics lose capacitance under DC bias) |
| R12 / R13 | 73.2k / 13.3k, 1 % | VOUT = 1.23 V × (1 + 73.2/13.3) = 8.00 V |
| C14 | 330 pF | feed-forward across R12, required for stability; zero near 6 kHz |
| R14 | 100k | EN pull-down: the boost stays off until the firmware sets BOOST_EN |
| R15 + C5 | 4.7 Ω + 10 µF | RC filter between the boost output (C13) and the emitter (+8V); drops about 0.1 V at 26 mA |

With EN low, the LED supply still sits about one diode drop below 5 V,
which is under the LED's forward voltage, so the LED stays dark.

Layout: keep L1, D1, C13 and U1's GND pin close together (the switching loop),
over a solid ground plane, and keep R12, R13 and C14 right at U1's FB pin. Put the
boost away from the ADC and the detector connector.

## Check before building

1. **RAW pin.** Rook ties RAW and B+ together to its LiPo, so RAW is most likely
   the battery pin, not USB 5 V. Leave JP1 open and supply 5 V on J5. To be
   sure, measure RAW to GND with only USB plugged in: only if it reads about
   5 V could JP1 be closed to run everything from the Pro Micro's USB-C.
2. **Pin order of your Pro Micro.** The footprint has the same 26 pads as the
   Rook v0.4 footprint, which works with these boards (left: B−, P0.06, P0.08,
   GND, GND, P0.17 … P1.06; right: B+, RAW, GND, RST, VCC, P0.31 … P0.09). As on
   Rook, the extra P1.01, P1.02 and P1.07 pins at the far end aren't used.
3. **Display header order.** J3 follows the common GND, VCC, SCL, SDA, RES, DC,
   CS, BL. Check it against your modules.
4. **8 V boost.** Before connecting the emitter, set BOOST_EN high and check
   that +8V reads about 7.9 V (C13, the boost output, about 8.0 V).
5. **Signal levels.** The ADS1115 runs at 3.3 V, so its inputs must stay below
   about 3.6 V. The detector's op-amps run from 5 V. If any detector output can go
   above 3.3 V, add a divider. The 4.7 kΩ series resistors limit the current
   if one does.
6. **LED_ENABLE polarity.** On emitter v0.4, LED_ENABLE switches a MOSFET that
   grounds the current setpoint. **HIGH turns the LED off**; LOW or floating lets
   LED_DRIVE set the current. The firmware drives it that way.
7. **Cables.** J1 and J2 are both 6-pin JST PH, so the cables fit either port.
   **Swapping them can damage the detector**: the emitter port puts 8 V and
   5 V on detector op-amp outputs. Label the ports clearly on the silkscreen and
   use different-coloured cables. Ready-made PH cables are often reversed
   (pin 1 to pin 6); these need pin 1 to pin 1.

## Breadboard prototype

It can be wired on a breadboard first, with breakouts standing in for the
on-board chips: Pro Micro, an ADS1115 breakout (same chip as U3, with its own
pull-ups), TFT, a boost module set to 8 V in place of U1, two 6-pin JST PH pigtails, three
buttons, the 20k/10k divider, the 100k pull-down on LED_DRIVE and the 4.7 kΩ
series resistors on the ADC inputs. Follow the pin map. The Pro Micro's VCC can
power the TFT and ADS1115 for a first test instead of U2. The firmware in
[`firmware/uv-control/v0.4`](../../firmware/uv-control/v0.4) reads all four
channels, switches the LED and shows the values on the TFT.

## Next steps in KiCad

- **Schematic.** First draft, ERC clean. Circuit blocks (the 8 V boost, the ADC,
  the LED_DRIVE parts) are drawn with wires; blocks connect to each other through
  tags (global labels), as on the other boards. Adjust values and add anything
  the prototype shows is missing.
- **PCB.** `uv-control.kicad_pcb` still holds the v0.1_A+ layout. To start v0.4,
  open it and use *Tools > Update PCB from Schematic* with *Delete footprints
  with no symbols* ticked, or delete everything and start again. The board no
  longer has to match the cuvette holder, so its size and shape are free.
- **Silkscreen.** Label J1 DETECTOR and J2 EMITTER clearly, and write the
  version as `${VERSION}`.
- **New library parts** in `lib/`: symbol and footprint `ProMicro_nRF52840`: two rows of 13 pads,
  15.24 mm apart, with the same pad positions and 17.78 × 33.02 mm outline as
  Rook's `nice_nano_cleanest`.
